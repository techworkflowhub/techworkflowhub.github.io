n8n vs. Zapier vs. Make (2026): Real Pricing Math & Execution Limits

Updated November 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Choosing between n8n, Zapier, and Make rarely comes down to which platform connects to Slack or Google Sheets. All three handle standard SaaS integrations. What actually decides your monthly bill—and whether your automations silently stop mid-month—is how each platform meters usage: Zapier counts action tasks, Make counts every module operation, and n8n counts full workflow executions (or nothing at all if self-hosted).

Quick Takeaway:
  • Pick Zapier if your team is non-technical, needs plug-and-play access to niche SaaS apps, and runs low-volume, linear workflows.
  • Pick Make if you want visual multi-step branching on a ~\–\/month budget and understand how to control polling and iterators.
  • Pick n8n if your workflows contain 8+ steps, loop over arrays, call LLMs repeatedly, or need self-hosted execution without per-task billing.

The Real Pricing Math: A 6-Step AI & CRM Workflow

To see why identical workflows produce wildly different invoices, consider a realistic daily automation: a webhook receives a new lead, formats fields, enriches company data via an API, summarizes notes with an LLM, updates a CRM, and posts a Slack alert. That is 1 trigger + 5 action steps, running 2,000 times per month.

  • On Zapier: Triggers and formatters do not all bill equally, so roughly 4–5 action tasks count per run → 8,000–10,000 tasks/month. You outgrow the 750-task starter plan immediately and move into mid-tier pricing.
  • On Make: Every module execution counts (including the trigger and intermediate steps) → ~6 operations × 2,000 runs = 12,000 credits/month. That exceeds the 10,000-credit Core base tier slightly, requiring a ~\–\/mo adjustment, still far cheaper than Zapier.
  • On n8n Cloud Starter: Regardless of having 6 nodes—or looping over 25 line items inside one run—it counts as 2,000 executions, fitting inside the ~\–\/mo Starter plan (2,500 executions).
  • On Self-Hosted n8n: Software license is \ under the Sustainable Use License; a \–\/mo VPS handles tens of thousands of runs without per-step penalties.

Platform-by-Platform Breakdown & Who Should Skip Each

1. n8n: Best for Complex Logic, Loops, and High-Volume AI

n8n wins whenever workflows stop being straight lines. Looping over 50 rows from a spreadsheet or running multi-step AI agent chains costs just 1 execution on n8n Cloud, whereas per-operation tools multiply cost by every item in the loop.

Who should skip n8n: Solo operators who refuse to touch JSON key paths, webhooks, or Docker, or teams whose workflows only need 2–3 simple steps between mainstream SaaS apps.

2. Zapier: Best for Speed and Niche SaaS Coverage

Zapier still has the broadest native integration catalog and lowest friction for marketing and operations teams that want non-developers building automations safely.

Who should skip Zapier: Anyone relying on a free tier (the 2-step cap blocks real formatting and conditional paths) or scaling high-frequency data syncs where per-task overages punish growth.

3. Make: Best Visual Value Under 25,000 Operations

Make's Core tier (~10,000 credits for ~\–\.59/mo) delivers strong value for visual builders who need routers, error handlers, and multi-step flows without managing a Linux server.

Who should skip Make: Workflows using tight 1-minute polling schedules on quiet endpoints (each empty poll consumes credits) or massive array iterators that multiply module counts unexpectedly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is n8n really free if I self-host?

The Community Edition software license is free for personal and internal business use, with unlimited workflows and executions. However, you still pay for your own server infrastructure (typically \–\/month on a VPS) and remain responsible for PostgreSQL backups, TLS certificates, and version upgrades.

Why does Make burn credits faster than expected?

Unlike Zapier—where triggers and filters do not consume tasks—Make deducts credits for every module that runs, including polling triggers and array iterators. Switching polling triggers to instant webhooks wherever possible cuts wasted credits dramatically.

Which platform is easiest for beginners in 2026?

Zapier remains the easiest for linear integrations, followed by Make for visual branching. n8n is best approached once you are comfortable reading API responses and JSON payloads.
